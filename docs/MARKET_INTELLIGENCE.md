# Market Intelligence

PAISAI does not merely **report** market events — it **explains** them, and traces
their transmission all the way to the user's own portfolio. A headline is not
intelligence. Understanding *why* it matters, *to whom*, and *by how much* is.

---

## Report vs. explain

**Reporting** (insufficient):

> "The Federal Reserve increased interest rates."

**Explaining** (the PAISAI standard) — the same event, decomposed:

- **Why** the action was taken.
- **Who benefits** from it.
- **Who suffers** from it.
- **Impact on bonds.**
- **Impact on equities.**
- **Impact on real estate.**
- **Impact on currencies.**
- **Impact on crypto.**
- **Impact on the user's own portfolio** — the transmission made personal.

---

## Standards for market explanations

Because market commentary is where fabrication is most tempting and most harmful,
these explanations are held to the same contracts as everything else:

- **No fabricated data.** Rates, prices, statistics, and news are Verified from
  integrated providers or they are not stated. (See the No-Hallucination Policy.)
- **Mechanisms, not narratives.** The explanation shows the causal channel — e.g.
  how a higher discount rate lowers the present value of long-duration cash flows —
  rather than a just-so story.
- **Second-order effects are labelled as inference.** "Higher rates *tend to*
  pressure growth equities" is an inference with a stated mechanism, not a
  guaranteed outcome.
- **Personalisation is bounded by data.** The portfolio-level impact is
  **Calculated** from the user's actual holdings where available, and clearly
  marked **Estimated** or withheld where it is not.
- **No market timing calls.** The system explains transmission; it does not tell
  the user the market will rise or fall. Forward statements remain probabilistic
  and assumption-bound.

The purpose is always education: after reading a Market Intelligence explanation,
the user should understand the *machinery* of the event well enough to reason
about the next one themselves.
